In future nuclear fusion devices, it is expected that the plasma periphery, which includes both the edge and scrape-off layer regions, plays an important role on the overall performance, namely regarding its influence on overall plasma confinement, power exhaust, plasma-wall interactions or ashes removal
%cite
.
While the scrape-off layer region has been described by a drift-reduced fluid formalism
% cite
, such approximations are questionable when applied to the edge, which is a hotter and less collisional region of the plasma.
In order to simulate and properly describe the plasma in such conditions, an accurate Coulomb operator is required. Previous studies
% cite
have shown the possibility to derive a linearized version of this operator, but since turbulent effects are expected to dominate the edge, we prefer considering a nonlinear description. Especially, we focus on a recent derivation
% cite
\footnote{The following computation are found in greater detail in
%cite
.}
yielding an operator which can easily be implemented in numerical simulation codes.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%% [02] GK model and Coulomb operator %%
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The gyrokinetic description of plasmas is based on first principles, which introduces simplifying assumption to the evolution of the Boltzmann distribution $f_a(\vec{x},\vec{v},t)$ of a species $a$, given by the Vlasov equation,
\begin{equation}
	\pdv{f_a}{t} + \dot{\vec{x}}\cdot\pdv{f_a}{\vec{x}} + \dot{\vec{v}}\cdot\pdv{f_a}{\vec{v}} = \sum_b C(f_a,f_b),
\end{equation}
where $C(f_a,f_b)$ is the Coulomb collision operator between species $a$ and $b$. When small angle collisions dominate, this operator can be expanded as
\begin{equation}
	C(f_a,f_b) = L_{ab} \sum_{j,k} \pdv{v_k} \biggl[ \pdv{v_j}\biggl(\pdv{G_b}{v_k}{v_j} f_a\biggr) - 2\biggl(1+\frac{m_a}{m_b}\biggr)\pdv{H_b}{v_k} f_a \biggr],
\end{equation}
where  the Rosenbluth potential $G_b$ and $H_b$, satisfying $\grad{v}^2G_b=2H_b$ and $\grad{v}^2H_b=-4\pi f_b$ defined as
\begin{align}
	G_b &= \int f_b(\vec{v}') \abs{\vec{v}-\vec{v}'}\dd{\vec{v}'} \\
	H_b &= \int \frac{f_b(\vec{v}')}{\abs{\vec{v}-\vec{v}'}} \dd{\vec{v}'} \label{eq:rosenbluth_H}
\end{align}
We introduced $L_{ab}=q_a^2q_b^2\lambda_{ab}/(8\pi^2\epsilon_0^2m_a^2)=\nu_{ab}v_{tha}^3/n_b$, where $\lambda_{ab}$ and $\nu_{ab}$ the Coulomb logarithm, resp. collision frequency, between species $a$ and $b$. We also write $v_{tha}=\sqrt{2T_a/m_a}$, $q_a$, $m_a$ and $n_a$ the thermal speed, charge, mass and density of particles of species $a=e,i$.

In order to recast $f_a$ into an appropriate basis, such that the Coulomb operator can be cast as a function of moments of $f_a$, it is noted that the Rosenbluth potential exhibit spherical symmetry, allowing one to perform the following multipole expansion of the factor in \cref{eq:rosenbluth_H},
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{\abs{\vec{v}-\vec{v}'}} =
	\begin{dcases}
	\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\vec{v}')^l}{l!} \cdot \pdv[l]{\vec{v}}\biggl(\frac{1}{v}\biggr), & v'\leq v, \\
	\sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-\vec{v}')^l}{l!} \cdot \pdv[l]{(\vec{v}')} \biggl(\frac{1}{v'}\biggr), & v<v'.
	\end{dcases}
\end{align}
Considering the spherical harmonic tensor $\tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v})$,
\begin{equation}
	\tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}) = \frac{(-1)^lv^{2l+1}}{(2l-1)!!}\biggl(\pdv{\vec{v}}\biggr)^l\frac{1}{v},
\end{equation}
the Rosenbluth potential $H_b$ is rewritten as
\begin{align}
	H_b &= 2 \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2l-1)!!}{l!} \biggl(
	\int_{v>v'} f_b(\vec{v}') \frac{(\vec{v}')^l}{v^{2l+1}} \tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}) \dd{\vec{v}'}
	+
	\int_{v'\geq v} f_b(\vec{v}') \frac{(\vec{v})^l}{(v')^{2l+1}}\tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}') \dd{\vec{v}'}
	\biggr)
	\\
	&= 2 \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2l-1)!!}{l!} \tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}) \biggl(
	\frac{1}{(v^2)^{l+1/2}} \int_{v'<v} f_b(\vec{v}') \tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}')\dd{\vec{v}'}
	+
	\int_{v'\geq v} f_b(\vec{v}')\frac{\tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}')}{[(v')^2]^{l+1/2}} \dd{\vec{v}'}
	\biggr),
\end{align}
where we used that $\tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v})$ is symmetric and totally traceless to obtain the second equality.

The integration can be continued by expanding $f_b$ into a similar basis,
\begin{equation}
	\tensor{Y}^{lk}(\vec{v}) = \tensor{Y}^l(\vec{v}) \laguerreL[l+1/2]{k}(v^2),
\end{equation}
with $\laguerreL[l+1/2]{k}(v)$ the associated Laguerre polynomial:
\begin{align}
	\laguerreL[l+1/2]{k}(v) = \sum_{m=0}^{k}L_{km}^l v^l,\\
	L_{km}^l=\frac{(-1)^m(l+k+1/2)!}{(k-m)!(l+m+1/2)!m!}.
\end{align}
This basis can be shown to be complete, such that $f_b$ is rewritten in terms of its velocity moments,
\begin{align}
	f_b(\vec{v}) &= f_{Mb} \sum_{l,k=0}^{\infty} \tensor{Y}^{lk}\biggl(\frac{\vec{v}}{v_{thb}}\biggr) \frac{\tensor{M}_b^{lk}}{\sigma_k^l},
	\\
	\tensor{M}_b^{lk} &= \frac{1}{n_b} \int f_b(\vec{v}) \tensor{Y}^{lk}\biggl(\frac{\vec{v}}{v_{thb}}\biggr)\dd{\vec{v}},
	\\
	\sigma_k^l &= \frac{l!(l+k+1/2)!}{2^l(l+1/2)!k!},
\end{align}
where $f_{Mb}$ a Maxwellian distribution function, and $\sigma_k^l$ a normalization constant. These expressions can then be replaced in the equations for $H_b$ and $G_b$.

After a lengthy calculation, it can be shown that, linearizing for small perturbations yields
\begin{equation}
	C_{ab} = \sum_{p,j=0}^{\infty} \frac{f_{Ma}}{\sigma_j^p} \tensor{M}_a^{pj}\tensor{Y}^p(\vu*{v}) \nu_{ab}^{Tpj}
	+
	\sum_{l,k=0}^{\infty} \frac{f_{Ma}}{\sigma_j^p} \tensor{M}_b^{lk}\tensor{Y}^p(\vu*{v}) \nu_{ab}^{Flk},
\end{equation}
where there are enough new constants and indices such that I don't manage to explain them all at once.
These moments can themselves be expressed, within the framework of gyrokinetic theory, as a function of the gyrokinetic moments $N_a^{lk}$.

In order to reach such conclusions, we note that if the Coulomb operator (and the distribution function) were expanded in terms of spherical harmonics, it is principally due to the spherical symmetry of the Coulomb interaction (the Rosenbluth potentials also displayed a $1/r$ dependence). It could also be suggested that a more adapted expansion of the distribution function is to be found when working in gyrokinetic coordinates $\vec{Z}$:
\begin{equation}
	\langle f_a \rangle_\vec{R} = \sum_{p,j=0}^{\infty} \frac{N_a^{pj}}{\sqrt{2^pp!}}f_{Ma}\hermiteH{p}(s_{\parallel a})\laguerreL{j}(s_{\perp a}^2),
\end{equation}
with $\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle_\vec{R}$ the gyro-averaging operator, $s_{\parallel a}=v_{\parallel a}/v_{th\parallel a}$ and $s_{\perp a}=v_{\perp a}/v_{th\perp a}$ the normalized parallel and perpendicular velocities, and $\hermiteH{p}(x)$ the physicist's Hermite polynomial of order $p$, given by the Rodrigues' formula,
\begin{align}
	\hermiteH{p}(x) = (-1)^p e^{x^2} \dv[p]{x} e^{-x^2},
	\\
	\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \hermiteH{p}(x)\hermiteH{p'}(x)e^{-x^2} \dd{x}
	= 2^pp!\sqrt{\pi}\delta_{pp'},
\end{align}
and $\laguerreL{j}(x)$ the Laguerre polynomials of order $j$, defined by
\begin{align}
	\laguerreL{j}(x) = \frac{e^x}{j!} \dv[j]{x}(e^{-x}x^j),
	\\
	\int_{0}^{\infty} \laguerreL{j}(x)\laguerreL{j'}(x)e^{-x} \dd{x} = \delta_{jj'}
\end{align}
The Hermite-Laguerre polynomial basis being orthogonal, the guiding-center moments are written
\begin{equation}
	N_a^{pj} = \frac{1}{N_a} \int \frac{
	\hermiteH{p}(s_{\parallel a})\laguerreL{j}(s_{\perp a}^2) \langle f_{a} \rangle_{\vec{R}}
	}{\sqrt{2^pp!}} \frac{B}{m_a} \dd{\mu} \dd{v_\parallel} \dd{\theta}.
\end{equation}
And don't ask me what $N_a$ is, I have no idea.
